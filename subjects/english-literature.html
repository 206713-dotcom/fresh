TOPIC 01

Imagery

Explain what an image makes you picture and how it changes your reading of a character, setting or theme.

Quick practice

What does ‘a cage of golden bars’ suggest about a wealthy character’s home?

Reveal answer

‘Golden’ suggests luxury, while ‘cage’ suggests confinement. The image implies that the character can be privileged and trapped at the same time.

Written practice

How might the contrast between “golden” and “cage” shape our view of the character’s wealth?

Compare with the example

“Golden” suggests luxury, while “cage” implies captivity. The contrast presents wealth as attractive but restrictive, so the reader may question what the character sacrifices to keep it.

TOPIC 02

Structure

Track shifts in tone, point of view or pace. Connect a shift to its effect on the reader.

Quick practice

A poem moves from ‘we’ to ‘I’ in its last stanza. Suggest one possible effect.

Reveal answer

The shift narrows the focus from a group to one speaker and may highlight isolation.

Written practice

Imagine the poem ends on the word “alone”. How could this ending change our reading of the earlier “we”?

Compare with the example

Ending on “alone” makes isolation the final impression. It can make the earlier collective “we” sound like a brief connection that the speaker no longer feels.

TOPIC 03

Drama

Stage directions guide performance. Consider how a pause, gesture or movement changes the audience’s interpretation.

Quick practice

A character says ‘Of course’ after a long pause. How might the pause change the line?

Reveal answer

The delay can suggest reluctance or doubt, making an apparent agreement feel uncertain.

Written practice

Describe how an actor could deliver “Of course” to suggest resentment, and explain the effect.

Compare with the example

The actor could pause and stress “course” in a flat voice. That delivery turns apparent agreement into sarcasm and lets the audience hear the resentment the character avoids expressing directly.
