Q: [3] Explain why the atomic radius decreases from sodium to chlorine.
+ proton number / nuclear charge increases :: nuclear charge|more protons|proton number
+ electrons are added to the same shell / shielding is similar :: same shell|similar shielding|shielding
+ so outer electrons are attracted more strongly and pulled closer :: stronger|more strongly|pulled closer|closer
A: Across the period the number of protons increases, while electrons go into the same shell, so shielding stays similar. The outer electrons feel a greater attraction to the nucleus and are pulled closer, so the radius decreases.
Q: [2] Explain why Mg²⁺ is smaller than Na⁺ even though both have 10 electrons.
+ Mg²⁺ has more protons (12 vs 11) :: more protons|12|greater nuclear charge
+ so stronger attraction for the same number of electrons :: stronger|more strongly|closer
A: Both are 2,8, but Mg²⁺ has 12 protons and Na⁺ only 11. The greater nuclear charge attracts the same number of electrons more strongly, pulling them closer.
Q: [2] Describe the paths of a beam of neutrons and a beam of protons through an electric field.
+ neutrons pass straight through / undeflected :: neutron & straight|undeflected|not deflected
+ protons bend towards the negative plate :: proton & negative
A: Neutrons have no charge, so they pass straight through undeflected. Protons are positive, so they are deflected towards the negative plate (less than electrons would be, because they are heavier).

Q: [2] Explain why isotopes of an element have identical chemical properties.
+ same number of electrons / same electronic configuration :: same & electron|configuration
+ chemical reactions involve electrons, not neutrons :: electrons & react|reaction|chemical

Q: [2] Explain the difference between relative isotopic mass and relative atomic mass.
+ relative isotopic mass: mass of one isotope's atom relative to 1/12 of ¹²C :: isotop & one|single|particular

A: Relative isotopic mass is the mass of an atom of one particular isotope relative to 1/12 of the mass of a ¹²C atom. Relative atomic mass is the weighted average mass of the atoms of an element, taking account of the abundances of all its isotopes, on the same scale.

A: Density (or rate of diffusion, melting or boiling point) differs, because atoms of different isotopes contain different numbers of neutrons and so have different masses.

Q: [2] Explain why the 4s subshell fills before 3d, but 4s electrons are lost first when transition metals form ions.
+ 4s is lower in energy than 3d in the empty atom, so it fills first :: lower energy|fills first|lower
+ once 3d is occupied, 4s is higher in energy / outermost, so it is lost first :: 3d is lower|4s is higher|outer|further

A: Electrons fill orbitals of equal energy singly, with parallel spins, before they pair up. In carbon, the two 2p electrons occupy two different 2p orbitals, each unpaired.

A: After the 3s electron is lost, the next electron must come from the full second shell (2p), which is much closer to the nucleus and much less shielded, so far more energy is needed.

Q: [2] Explain why atom economy and percentage yield are different measures.
+ atom economy: proportion of reactant mass ending up in the desired product (from the equation) :: atom economy & desired|useful|equation
+ yield: how much product is actually obtained compared with the theoretical maximum :: yield & actual|obtained|theoretical
A: Atom economy is calculated from the balanced equation: the mass of desired product as a percentage of the total mass of reactants. Percentage yield compares the amount of product actually obtained with the theoretical maximum. A reaction can have a high yield but a low atom economy.
Q: [2] Explain why the volumes of gases in a reaction (at the same temperature and pressure) are in the same ratio as the moles in the equation.
+ equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of molecules (Avogadro) :: equal volume|same volume|avogadro
+ so volume is proportional to moles :: proportional|ratio
A: At the same temperature and pressure, equal volumes of all gases contain the same number of molecules, so the volume of a gas is proportional to the number of moles.
