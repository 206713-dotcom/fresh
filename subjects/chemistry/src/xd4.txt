Q: [3] Explain the variation in melting point from sodium to aluminium and then to silicon.
+ Na to Al: metallic bonding gets stronger (more delocalised electrons, smaller, higher charge ions) :: metallic & stronger|more delocalised|charge
+ silicon has a giant covalent structure :: giant covalent|macromolecular
+ many strong covalent bonds must be broken, so highest melting point :: strong covalent|many bonds|most energy|highest
A: From Na to Al, the metal ions become smaller and more highly charged and there are more delocalised electrons per atom, so metallic bonding gets stronger and the melting point rises. Silicon has a giant covalent structure; melting it requires breaking many strong covalent bonds, so it has the highest melting point in the period.

A: These elements exist as small molecules (P₄, S₈, Cl₂) or single atoms (Ar). Melting only needs the weak London forces between the particles to be overcome, which needs little energy.

Q: [3] Explain how oxides of nitrogen are formed in a car engine and how they contribute to acid rain.
+ high temperature in the engine makes N₂ react with O₂: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO :: high temperature & n2 + o2|2no
+ NO is oxidised to NO₂ in the air: 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ :: 2no + o2|no2
+ NO₂ dissolves in rain to form nitric acid (or catalyses SO₂ → SO₃) :: nitric acid|hno3|so3|catalys
A: In a car engine, the high temperature provides enough energy for nitrogen and oxygen from the air to react: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO. In the atmosphere NO is oxidised: 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂. NO₂ reacts with water and oxygen to form nitric acid, and it also catalyses the oxidation of SO₂ to SO₃, which forms sulfuric acid; both cause acid rain.
Q: [2] Explain why ammonia is a base, using its structure.
+ the nitrogen atom has a lone pair :: lone pair
+ which it uses to accept a proton (H⁺) / form a dative bond, giving NH₄⁺ :: accept|proton|h+|dative
A: The nitrogen atom in ammonia has a lone pair of electrons, which it can use to accept a proton (H⁺) by forming a dative covalent bond, making NH₄⁺. A proton acceptor is a Brønsted–Lowry base.
