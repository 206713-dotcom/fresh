Q: [3] Describe how a primary, a secondary and a tertiary alcohol can be distinguished using acidified potassium dichromate(VI).
+ warm each with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ :: dichromate|k2cr2o7|cr2o7
+ primary and secondary: orange to green; tertiary: stays orange :: green & orange|tertiary & no change|stays orange
+ distinguish primary from secondary by testing the product: aldehyde (from primary) gives positive Tollens' / Fehling's; ketone does not :: tollens|fehling|silver mirror|aldehyde
A: Warm each alcohol with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Primary and secondary alcohols turn it from orange to green; a tertiary alcohol leaves it orange. To tell primary from secondary, distil off the product: the aldehyde from a primary alcohol gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent (or a red precipitate with Fehling's), whereas the ketone from a secondary alcohol does not.
