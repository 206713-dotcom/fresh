Q: [3] Describe tests to show that a solid is copper(II) carbonate.
+ add dilute acid: effervescence; gas turns limewater milky (CO₂, carbonate) :: limewater|milky|carbon dioxide|effervesc
+ dissolve and add NaOH: light blue precipitate (Cu²⁺) :: light blue|blue precipitate
+ or flame test blue-green / aqueous ammonia dark blue in excess :: blue-green|dark blue|deep blue
A: Add dilute acid: it fizzes and the gas turns limewater milky, showing carbonate. To the solution formed, add aqueous sodium hydroxide: a light blue precipitate forms, showing Cu²⁺ (or with excess aqueous ammonia, a dark blue solution forms; a flame test gives blue-green).
Q: [2] Describe how to distinguish between aqueous iron(II) sulfate and aqueous iron(III) sulfate.
+ add aqueous sodium hydroxide (or ammonia) :: sodium hydroxide|naoh|ammonia
+ iron(II): green precipitate; iron(III): red-brown precipitate :: green & red-brown|red brown|brown
A: Add aqueous sodium hydroxide to each. Iron(II) sulfate gives a green precipitate; iron(III) sulfate gives a red-brown precipitate.
