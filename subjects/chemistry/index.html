Live model: what you are looking at

An atomic orbital, drawn live. Each dot is one place where the electron could be found. The 24,000 dots are picked at random from |ψ|², the probability density of the electron in a hydrogen atom, so the cloud is densest where the electron is most likely to be.

Colour shows the sign of the wavefunction ψ: blue where it is positive, orange where it is negative. Where the two colours meet there is a node, and the electron is never found there. Drag to turn the atom. Pick another orbital and the same dots rearrange.

Honest caveats: these are the orbitals of hydrogen, which has one electron. In bigger atoms the shapes stay similar but the sizes and energies change. A real orbital has no edge, it just fades out: textbooks draw the surface that holds about 90% of the cloud. The turning is only the camera. The electron is not going round like a planet.
