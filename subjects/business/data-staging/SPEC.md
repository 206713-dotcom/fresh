## Strict 0264 whitelist (only these may be tested)

### Unit 1: Understanding Business Activity
1.1 Business activity: needs, wants, scarcity, opportunity cost; factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise); specialisation and division of labour; business purpose; added value and how to increase it.
1.2 Economic sectors: primary, secondary, tertiary sectors; changes in sector importance (industrialisation, de-industrialisation); private sector vs public sector in mixed economies.
1.3 Enterprise, business growth and size: characteristics of successful entrepreneurs; contents of a business plan; why/how governments support start-ups (grants, training, tax incentives, low-cost premises); methods of measuring business size (number of people employed, value of output, value of sales, value of capital employed) and their limitations; why some businesses grow and others remain small; internal (organic) growth vs external growth (horizontal integration, forward vertical integration, backward vertical integration); problems of business growth and how to overcome them; why new/established businesses fail.
1.4 Types of business organisation: sole traders, partnerships, private limited companies (Ltd), public limited companies (Plc), franchises, joint ventures; unincorporated vs limited companies; business organisations in the public sector (public corporations).
1.5 Business objectives and stakeholder objectives: survival, growth, profit, market share, providing a service to the community / social enterprise; why objectives change; stakeholder groups and why their objectives conflict; private vs public sector enterprise objectives.

### Unit 2: People in Business
2.1 Motivating employees: importance of a well-motivated workforce; Maslow's hierarchy of needs; Taylor's scientific management; Herzberg's two-factor theory (hygiene factors and motivators); financial rewards (wage/time rate, salary, piece rate, bonus, commission, profit sharing, share ownership); non-financial methods (job enrichment, job rotation, teamworking, training, opportunities for promotion).
2.2 Organisation and management: simple organisational charts (hierarchy, span of control, chain of command, tall vs flat structures, delayering); roles of management (planning, organising, coordinating, commanding, controlling); delegation (advantages/disadvantages for manager and worker); leadership styles (autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire ONLY); trade unions (what a trade union is; effects of employees being union members).
2.3 Recruitment, selection and training: recruitment process (job analysis, job description, person specification, job advertisement); internal vs external recruitment; selection methods (application forms, CVs, interviews, testing/assessment); part-time vs full-time employees; importance of training; induction training, on-the-job training, off-the-job training; workforce reduction (downsizing, dismissal vs redundancy, factors in deciding who to make redundant); legal controls over employment contracts (unfair dismissal, discrimination, health and safety, legal minimum wage).
2.4 Internal and external communication: effective communication and its importance; communication methods (verbal, written, visual, electronic/IT) and choosing appropriate methods; communication barriers and how to overcome them.

### Unit 3: Marketing
3.1 Marketing, competition and the customer: role of marketing (identifying/satisfying customer needs, maintaining customer loyalty, building customer relationships); market changes (why consumer spending patterns change, power/importance of changing customer needs, why markets become more competitive, how businesses respond); mass vs niche marketing; market segmentation (demographic, geographic, income/age/gender/lifestyle).
3.2 Market research: role of market research; market-orientated businesses; primary vs secondary research; primary methods (questionnaires, interviews, focus groups, observation); secondary methods (government statistics, internet, market research reports, internal records); sampling — **random sampling and quota sampling ONLY**; factors influencing accuracy of market research data; presentation and use of results.
3.3 Marketing mix:
- Product: costs/benefits of developing new products; brand image and impact on sales/loyalty; role of packaging; product life cycle (development, introduction, growth, maturity, saturation, decline) and extension strategies.
- Price: pricing methods (**cost-plus, competitive, penetration, price skimming, promotional, dynamic pricing**); price elasticity of demand as a concept (price-elastic vs price-inelastic demand and effect on pricing decisions — conceptual only, NO calculation).
- Place: channels of distribution (direct to consumer, retailer, wholesaler, agent); e-commerce (opportunities and threats to businesses and consumers).
- Promotion: aims of promotion; advertising (informative, persuasive); sales promotion (price reductions, gifts, BOGOF, competitions, point-of-sale displays, free samples); personal selling; public relations / sponsorship; technology and the marketing mix (social media, viral marketing, e-commerce).
3.4 Marketing strategy: recommending/justifying a marketing strategy in a given situation; legal controls over marketing (misleading promotion, faulty/dangerous goods, weights and measures); entering new markets abroad (opportunities, problems, methods to overcome: joint ventures, licensing, international franchising, localising).

### Unit 4: Operations Management
4.1 Production of goods and services: managing resources effectively (difference between production and productivity, benefits of increasing efficiency, why businesses hold inventories); lean production (**kaizen, just-in-time [JIT], cell production**); methods of production (**job, batch, flow production**); how technology has changed production (automation, mechanisation, CAD, CAM, CIM, EPOS).
4.2 Costs, scale of production and break-even analysis: fixed, variable, total and average costs; economies of scale (purchasing, marketing, financial, managerial, technical) and diseconomies of scale (poor communication, low morale, slow decision-making); break-even analysis (break-even output = Fixed Costs / (Price − Variable Cost per unit); margin of safety = Actual Output − Break-even Output; interpreting break-even charts; limitations of break-even analysis).
4.3 Achieving quality production: why quality is important; **quality control vs quality assurance**; **Total Quality Management (TQM)**.
4.4 Location decisions: factors influencing location of manufacturing, service sector, and retailing businesses; legal controls and trade blocs on location; factors influencing relocation to another country.

### Unit 5: Financial Information and Decisions
5.1 Business finance: needs and sources: need for finance (start-up capital, working capital, capital expenditure); internal sources (retained profit, sale of existing assets, owners' savings); external sources (issue of shares, bank loans, selling debentures, factoring of debts, grants/subsidies, micro-finance, crowdfunding); short-term vs long-term finance; factors influencing choice of finance.
5.2 Cash-flow forecasting and working capital: importance of cash and cash-flow forecasting; interpreting/amending/completing simple cash-flow forecasts (inflows, outflows, net cash flow, opening/closing balance); overcoming short-term cash-flow problems (bank loans/overdrafts, delaying payments to suppliers, asking debtors to pay faster, delaying/cancelling capital expenditure); working capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.
5.3 Income statements: importance of profit; difference between profit and cash; components of an income statement (Revenue, Cost of Sales, Gross Profit, Expenses, Profit/Net Profit, Retained Profit).
5.4 Statement of financial position: main elements (Non-current assets, Current assets, Current liabilities, Non-current liabilities, Working capital, Capital employed, Shareholders' equity/funds).
5.5 Analysis of accounts: profitability ratios (**Gross Profit Margin, Profit Margin / Net Profit Margin, Return on Capital Employed [ROCE]**); liquidity ratios (**Current Ratio, Acid Test Ratio**); uses and limitations of ratio analysis; why/how accounts are used by stakeholders.

### Unit 6: External Influences on Business Activity
6.1 Economic issues: business cycle (**growth, boom, recession, slump**); government economic objectives; effects of changes in taxes (income tax, corporation tax, indirect taxes/VAT, import tariffs), government spending, and interest rates on business activity; effects of changes in employment, inflation and economic growth on business.
6.2 Environmental and ethical issues: environmental impacts of business; externalities (external costs and external benefits); sustainable development; environmental pressures and opportunities; legal controls over environmental impact; ethical issues (conflicts between profit and ethics).
6.3 Business and the international economy: globalisation (reasons, opportunities, threats); import tariffs and quotas; multinational companies (MNCs — benefits to business, impact on stakeholders/host country); exchange rates (appreciation and depreciation effects on exporters and importers).

## Numbers discipline (Units 4 & 5)
Break-even output = Fixed Costs / (Price − Variable cost per unit). Margin of safety = Actual output − Break-even output. Revenue = Price × Quantity. Profit = Revenue − Cost of sales − Expenses. Gross Profit Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100. Profit Margin = Profit / Revenue × 100. ROCE = Profit / Capital Employed × 100. Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities. Acid Test = (Current Assets − Inventories) / Current Liabilities. Working capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities. Capital employed = Non-current assets + (Current assets − Current liabilities). ALL arithmetic in stems/answers must be correct.
