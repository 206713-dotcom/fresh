What you are looking at

A single twistor, as Roger Penrose draws it. A twistor of this kind is a family of light rays that twist around each other, called a Robinson congruence. Freeze it at one instant and the directions of the rays line up along these circles: the fibres of the Hopf map of the 3-sphere, projected into ordinary space. They lie on nested tori around one round core circle, and the straight line through the middle is one of them too, a circle that closes at infinity. Each circle is linked once with every other.

Honest caveats: in Penrose’s picture the whole pattern also drifts along the axis at the speed of light, which is not shown. The slow turn and the circles sliding round their tori only show the shape (the sliding carries the family onto itself). The colours only tell the circles apart. The bright points run along the circles, as light runs along a ray.
