    Historical skills exercises

    Try each question before revealing the answer. Check your exam-year syllabus for the full topic list.

    TOPIC 01

    Causes

    Distinguish long-term conditions from short-term triggers. Explain how each factor contributed to the outcome.

    Quick practice

    An alliance system existed for years before a crisis led to war. How could the alliances have contributed?

    Reveal answer

    Alliance commitments could widen a local conflict by drawing in countries that supported the original participants.

    Written practice

    Explain the difference between a long-term cause and a trigger, using alliances and a crisis as your examples.

    Compare with the example

    Alliances can be a long-term cause because they shape how states react to one another over years. A crisis is a trigger if it creates the immediate decision to mobilise or fight.

    TOPIC 02

    Source evaluation

    Use a source’s content, origin, audience and purpose to judge how useful it is for a specific question.

    Quick practice

    What can a wartime recruitment poster tell a historian about recruitment?

    Reveal answer

    It shows the arguments and images used to persuade people. Actual enlistment figures would help assess how recruitment changed.

    Written practice

    How could you check whether a recruitment poster persuaded people to enlist?

    Compare with the example

    Compare enlistment records from before and after its distribution and look for testimony from recruits. Consider other changes at the same time, such as a new conscription law or a major battle.

    TOPIC 03

    Comparing explanations

    Support each explanation with evidence and use a clear criterion, such as impact or timing, to justify your judgement.

    Quick practice

    What makes an explanation of a policy’s failure stronger?

    Reveal answer

    It connects specific evidence to the outcome and explains why the factor mattered compared with other causes.

    Written practice

    How would you decide whether weak planning or a lack of money mattered more to a policy’s failure?

    Compare with the example

    Use evidence about when each problem appeared and which outcomes it affected. Explain whether better planning could have helped with the funds available and test that explanation against the evidence.
